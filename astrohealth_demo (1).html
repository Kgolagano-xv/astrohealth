Crew buddy alerts: who is told what

If a crewmate declines, the people nearby can help, without exposing private health data.

GREENNobody. All systems nominal.
YELLOWThe astronaut only: a private nudge.
ORANGEAstronaut and crew medical officer. A buddy gets a gentle "check in today" note with no medical details.
REDWhole crew alarm plus Mission Control (simulated). The nearest crewmate is directed to respond with the checklist.
CHECK-IN FLAGCrew medical officer only. Never shared with the crew unless the astronaut chooses.
BUDDY CHECK

Please check in on D. Okafor today.

He may be unwell. Keep an eye on him and tell the crew medical officer if you are worried. No medical details are shared.

Rules we propose

  • Agreed by the crew before the mission.
  • Minimum information, and the astronaut can see who was told.
  • Why tiered: if every decline is shared, astronauts may hide symptoms or avoid sensors (National Academies, 2001).
  • To be agreed with flight surgeons and NASA medical-privacy rules.

Mockup, MOCK DATA. A design proposal to test, not an existing NASA procedure.

How we are different

We add a layer on top of what NASA already does. We do not replace it.

What the public record shows

  • Astronauts often eat roughly 20-30% fewer calories than they need, and lose weight.
  • Weight loss is linked to extra bone and muscle loss. Bone loss is about 1-1.5% per month.
  • Ground doctors review weight and counsel astronauts. Crew log food in an app.

What AstroHealth adds

  • Automatic data where possible, so tired astronauts do not have to log everything by hand.
  • One combined picture: vitals, sleep, stress, isolation, diet, exercise and weight.
  • Personal baselines and trends that warn early during the mission, not at the end.
  • Plain-language what / why / what to do, for the astronaut and the ground team.

Based on public NASA nutrition-science reports. NASA's current internal tools may already do some of this; we add the combining and early-warning layer.

Summary report for the flight surgeon

What it can do

Catch avoidable decline sooner, such as under-eating, and flag poor sleep, stress and isolation early. Weight loss is linked to extra bone and muscle loss, so acting earlier may help.

What it cannot do

Stop the bone and muscle loss caused by microgravity itself. Whether earlier nudges reduce losses is our hypothesis, tested on simulated crews, not a proven result.

Limits and next steps

Limits we state openly

  • All data is simulated. No real NASA astronaut data is used.
  • A research prototype, not a medical device. It flags changes for people to review.
  • Thresholds are placeholders until checked against published sources.
  • Stress, isolation, mood, diet intake and some blood markers are simulated or estimated from proxies, not measured directly.
  • Monitoring cannot stop the bone and muscle loss caused by microgravity itself.

Next steps
